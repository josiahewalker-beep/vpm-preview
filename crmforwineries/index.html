An outside read on CRM for Wineries.

This is a brief AI-assisted analysis I put together from public sources only: your site, your Commerce7 listing, G2 and recent industry reports. You know the market and your clients far better than this can. I'm sharing it because an outside read sometimes connects things that are hard to see from inside the work. Take what's useful and set aside the rest.

The outside read: three pressures

  1. The ground under the product has moved.

    With WineDirect's software business and WinePulse now part of Commerce7, reporting and club analytics are becoming things Commerce7 customers get from Commerce7. Your public description still leads with those capabilities, which puts it in a space your platform partner now covers. The part of your offer Commerce7 doesn't touch, wholesale alongside DTC, is the most defensible and the least visible from outside.

    WineDirect SaaS ↗WinePulse ↗

  2. The buyer for that part is specific, and has little to evaluate yet.

    One system for DTC and wholesale matters most to a winery with a sales team, several distributors and a CFO who buys on evidence. From outside, that person can't yet find named work, results, a sense of scope and cost, or who they'd be working with. Competitors on the same marketplace show those specifics.

    Assemblage listing ↗Vinosmith ↗

  3. The market is creating demand nobody has claimed.

    Nearly one in four wineries lost a primary distributor (BMO, 2026), and DTC shipments fell 15% by volume in 2025 (Sovos). Wineries now selling to trade themselves need what you already build: order-taking, invoicing, compliance and inventory across locations. Nobody has packaged or named that for them yet.

    BMO 2026 ↗Sovos DTC report ↗

Three ways to position the business

A

The other half of Commerce7

“Keep Commerce7 for DTC. We bring wholesale in beside it.” Rides Commerce7's growth and makes you a natural referral from its team.

Trade-off: ties your fortunes to one platform.

How many of your clients came through Commerce7?

B

One system for the whole winery

Lead with the questions that need both channels: which sommeliers are club members, where placements were lost near members, whether a by-the-glass placement lifts DTC nearby, what inventory looks like across every warehouse.

Trade-off: needs to be shown working, with a walkthrough and one client story.

Which of these do your clients actually use?

C

The self-distribution partner

A defined package for wineries that lost or left a distributor. A new market with an urgent, clear trigger.

Trade-off: new scope to define, support and price.

Is this work you want more of?

The redesign uses B as the main story, keeps A on the integration page, and offers C as a package. Which one leads is your call.

What makes any of them work

  • One named client with one result.
  • A first package with a starting price and timeline.
  • You on the page, so a buyer knows who they'd be talking to.

How depletions reach Salesforce today, which of those questions your current build answers, typical scope and pricing, and which clients could be named.

The problem

Two businesses under one roof that don't talk to each other.

DTC lives in Commerce7. Wholesale lives in spreadsheets and distributor depletion reports, each distributor sending its own format. Nobody sees the whole business in one place.

DTC

Lives in Commerce7

  • Club memberstiers, shipments
  • Tasting roomvisits, POS
  • Ecommerceorders
  • Allocation listsreleases

Wholesale

Lives in inboxes and spreadsheets

  • Depletion reportsone format per distributor
  • Trade accountson- and off-premise
  • Orders and invoicesthree-tier
  • Warehouse inventoryby distributor

So decisions about channel mix, pricing, inventory and trade spend get made on half the data.

NoteThe differentiator, made concrete. Commerce7 sees consumers. Wholesale tools such as Vinosmith and depletion platforms see trade accounts. Each question crosses the line, so only a unified system answers it.

Questions

What buyers ask first.

Does this replace Commerce7?

No. Commerce7 stays your DTC platform, and its reporting and churn tools keep working as they do. The integration brings Commerce7's data into Salesforce so it sits next to your wholesale business, and sends inventory changes back.

Do we need Salesforce already?

You need Salesforce Sales Cloud licences, billed by Salesforce and not included in our packages. If you already have Salesforce, we work inside your existing org.

Every distributor sends depletions in a different format. Is that a problem?

That is the normal case. Bring a few recent reports to the demo and we will show you where each field lands in Salesforce.

Commerce7 + Salesforce

What syncs, which way, and what it lets you see.

The integration listens to Commerce7 webhooks, so DTC activity reaches Salesforce as it happens rather than in a nightly export. Changes made in Salesforce, such as inventory quantities, go back to Commerce7. Your wholesale data joins it in Salesforce.
